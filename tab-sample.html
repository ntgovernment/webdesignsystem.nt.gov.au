Business Licensing in the Northern Territory

Everything you need to know about obtaining and maintaining business licenses in the NT

Overview

Operating a business in the Northern Territory requires compliance with various licensing requirements. This guide provides comprehensive information about business licenses, permits, and registrations needed for different types of businesses.

Important Notice

Starting a business without the required licenses may result in fines, penalties, or legal action. Ensure you understand all requirements before commencing operations.

Who Needs a Business License?

Most businesses operating in the NT require some form of licensing or registration. The specific requirements depend on:

  • Type of business activity
  • Location of operations
  • Number of employees
  • Industry sector (e.g., food, construction, hospitality)
  • Whether you're operating from home or commercial premises

Common License Types

Food Business License

Required for restaurants, cafes, food trucks, and any business handling food.

Builder's License

Required for construction, building, and renovation work above specified thresholds.

Liquor License

Required for businesses selling, serving, or distributing alcohol.

Accommodation License

Required for hotels, motels, hostels, and short-term rental properties.

Tip

Many businesses require multiple licenses. Use our License Finder Tool to identify all licenses you need for your specific business type.

How to Apply

The application process varies depending on the license type, but generally follows these steps:

  1. Determine License Requirements

    Identify which licenses and permits your business needs. Consider consulting with a business advisor or using our online license finder tool.

  2. Gather Required Documents

    Collect all necessary documentation including business registration, identification, proof of premises, and any industry-specific certificates.

  3. Complete Application Forms

    Fill out the application forms accurately and completely. Incomplete applications will be returned and delay processing.

  4. Pay Application Fees

    Submit the required fees with your application. Fees vary by license type and are generally non-refundable, even if your application is not approved.

  5. Submit Application

    Submit your application online through the NT Business Portal or by mail to the relevant licensing authority.

  6. Await Assessment

    Applications are assessed by the relevant authority. Processing times vary from 2-12 weeks depending on license type and application complexity.

  7. Receive License

    Once approved, you'll receive your license certificate. Some licenses must be displayed at your business premises.

Documents You'll Need

  • Proof of identity – Driver's license or passport
  • Business registration – ABN and business name registration
  • Premises documentation – Lease agreement or proof of ownership
  • Floor plans – For food and retail premises
  • Insurance certificates – Public liability insurance
  • Qualifications – Industry certifications where required
  • Police clearances – For certain license types
Online Applications

Most licenses can be applied for online through the NT Business Portal. Online applications are processed faster and allow you to track application status in real-time.

Payment Methods

License fees can be paid by:

  • Credit or debit card (online applications)
  • BPAY
  • Cheque or money order (mail applications)
  • In person at NT Government service centers

Refund Policy

Application fees are generally non-refundable, even if your application is withdrawn or refused. However, you may be eligible for a refund in certain circumstances:

  • License authority error or duplication
  • Overpayment of fees
  • Application withdrawn before assessment begins

Processing Time Factors

Several factors can affect how long your application takes to process:

  • Application completeness – Incomplete applications are returned
  • License complexity – Some licenses require site inspections or consultations
  • Public notification period – Some licenses require public comment periods
  • Peak periods – Applications lodged in December-January may take longer
  • Additional checks – Police checks, background verification can add time

License Renewals and Compliance

Most business licenses are not permanent and must be renewed periodically. Maintaining compliance with license conditions is essential to avoid penalties and ensure uninterrupted operations.

Renewal Process

License renewal reminders are typically sent 4-6 weeks before expiration. To renew your license:

  1. Check your license expiry date
  2. Review any changes to licensing requirements
  3. Update your business details if changed
  4. Pay the renewal fee
  5. Submit any required updated documentation
Don't Let Your License Lapse

Operating with an expired license is illegal and can result in fines up to $10,000 and immediate closure orders. Set calendar reminders well before your expiry date.

Compliance Requirements

License holders must comply with various conditions depending on the license type:

General Compliance

  • Display licenses at business premises where required
  • Maintain required insurance coverage
  • Keep accurate business records
  • Notify authorities of business changes
  • Allow inspections by licensing officers
  • Maintain health and safety standards

Industry-Specific Compliance

Food Businesses:

  • Food safety training for all food handlers
  • Regular health inspections
  • Temperature monitoring and record keeping
  • Pest control programs

Licensed Builders:

  • Maintain required insurance levels
  • Complete continuing professional development
  • Comply with building codes and standards
  • Maintain complaint handling procedures

Liquor Licensees:

  • Responsible service of alcohol training
  • Comply with trading hour restrictions
  • Maintain incident registers
  • Follow advertising regulations

Inspections and Audits

Licensed businesses may be subject to inspections by NT Government licensing officers. Inspections can be:

  • Scheduled – Advance notice provided
  • Unannounced – Random compliance checks
  • Complaint-triggered – Following public complaints
  • Incident-based – After accidents or violations

Penalties for Non-Compliance

Failure to comply with license conditions can result in:

  • Warning notices
  • Infringement notices and fines
  • License suspension
  • License cancellation
  • Prosecution and criminal penalties
